# Synthetic Statistical Behavior of the Utilities {#sec:synthetic-statistics}

The preceding sections define what each utility computes. This section reports what those utilities *do* on their declared synthetic inputs, computed by the seeded runner in `src/experiments/`. Every quantity here is synthetic: inputs are fixed or seeded constructions, replication is resampling over seeded runs, and intervals summarize replication-to-replication variation of the computation itself. No corpus, participant, physiological, hardware, or deployed-agent evidence is involved, and none of these numbers estimates a property of any language or brain.

The runner executes configured arms with a seeded PCG64 generator (seed ${experiment_seed}, ${experiment_replicates} replicates per study) and emits a schema-annotated result file whose configuration hash is `${experiment_config_sha256}`; the manuscript quotes only registry-declared identifiers from that file through injection. Replicate-level means carry two-sided ${ci_level_percent}% normal-approximation intervals (normal quantile ${ci_z_score}); point maxima and residuals are reported as descriptive bounds without intervals. There are no p-values, significance labels, or comparisons with empirical work in this section, and all configured arms are reported without multiplicity adjustment.

## Calibration of quantile coverage {#sec:exp-calibration}

For a finite discrete return law, the exact coverage target of a quantile level is its distribution function $F(Q(\tau))$, which need not equal $\tau$ under the discrete generalized inverse; that atom gap is a separate descriptive quantity. With ${experiment_calibration_levels} levels and ${experiment_calibration_observations} observations per replicate, observed coverage of the stored quantiles is compared with that exact target. The mean absolute deviation from the exact discrete-law target is ${exp_calibration_coverage_error_mean} with interval [${exp_calibration_coverage_error_mean_ci95_low}, ${exp_calibration_coverage_error_mean_ci95_high}]; this deviation quantifies Monte Carlo sampling error of the empirical coverage estimate around the exact discrete target, not distance from a continuous coverage convention. The largest per-replicate deviation is ${exp_calibration_coverage_error_max_replicate}, and the largest per-level gap between mean observed coverage and the mean exact target is ${exp_calibration_level_target_gap_max}. The mean exact atom gap $F(Q(\tau)) - \tau$ itself is ${exp_calibration_atom_gap_mean}, and its largest value is ${exp_calibration_atom_gap_max}; both are deterministic discretization properties of the stored atoms and carry no sampling error. These diagnostics are properties of the stored representation and the seeded sampling scheme, not estimates from data.

## Aggregation and localization diagnostics {#sec:exp-aggregation}

A final study mirrors the post-extraction aggregation and localization ablations of the Democritus publication [@mahadevan2026democritus] on a seeded synthetic paraphrase-family corpus. Claim records are generated in families whose members share the exact canonical cause, effect, relation, polarity, and regime while varying only qualifier phrasing, and selected families additionally straddle two regime labels with a polarity-flipped duplicate on each side. Localization then groups records under exact canonical keys, so a record with an unspecified regime forms its own class and never merges with a regime-labeled one.

Three contrasts are reported against this corpus, all at the replicate sample unit with the same normal-approximation intervals as the studies above. The no-localization contrast compares the number of localized classes with the raw record count: the mean inflation reduction is ${exp_agg_inflation_reduction_mean} (dimensionless) with interval [${exp_agg_inflation_reduction_mean_ci95_low}, ${exp_agg_inflation_reduction_mean_ci95_high}]. The regime-projection ablation recounts polarity conflicts after projecting the regime away; the paired mean difference between the regime-resolved count and the projected count is ${exp_agg_regime_projection_lost_mean} conflicts with interval [${exp_agg_regime_projection_lost_mean_ci95_low}, ${exp_agg_regime_projection_lost_mean_ci95_high}], where a positive value counts regime-resolved conflicts that collapse into merged ones under the projection. The closure diagnostic reports the mean triangle-closure ratio of the within-class compatibility graphs, ${exp_agg_triangle_closure_ratio_mean} (dimensionless) with interval [${exp_agg_triangle_closure_ratio_mean_ci95_low}, ${exp_agg_triangle_closure_ratio_mean_ci95_high}]; following the cited publication's own account of its compatibility complex, this ratio is a finite pairwise-closure statistic on observed pairs, not a horn-filling test in the nerve of a groupoid. Two descriptive counts complete the block without intervals: the regime-resolved polarity-conflict count ${exp_agg_regime_conflicts_detected_mean} and the mean localized class count ${exp_agg_localized_class_count_mean}.

The boundary of this subsection is narrower than the rest of the section. Every corpus is synthetic, so no property of any real claim population is measured, and no real-corpus claim is made. The merge correctness of localization is unvalidated against any annotated standard; the cited publication itself reports such an independent post-extraction benchmark as its most important next step [@mahadevan2026democritus]. The numbers above validate the bookkeeping behavior of the aggregation and localization utilities on known synthetic constructions, nothing more.

## Interpretation boundaries {#sec:exp-limits}

Three boundaries delimit this section. First, replication intervals summarize variation across seeded runs of deterministic code; they are not sampling distributions over any population. Second, every configured arm is reported without multiplicity adjustment, and the configurations themselves are synthetic choices, not estimated models. Third, agreement with closed forms and numerical gradients validates the implementation of a stated computation; it neither extends the mathematics beyond its assumptions nor converts any utility into a Bellman solver, a general inference engine, or a measurement model. The reproduction path for every number in this section is documented in [@sec:test-suite-inventory].
